# Report 3 — Literature update and research-gap analysis

## 3.1 Literature located

Searches used: Consensus (peer-reviewed index) and web search, on NN surrogates of homogenized stiffness in multiscale TO, Sobolev / derivative-informed training, physically constrained surrogates, uncertainty-aware surrogates, second-order solvers and benchmarking in TO, and follow-up work by the paper's authors. Only abstracts and landing pages were read, so the "not reported in the abstract" column is a statement about the abstract, not about the full paper. No citation below was written from memory alone; each has a link that was returned by a search.

## 3.2 Research gaps

Each gap lists the evidence in the paper, the supporting literature, why it is open, how to investigate it, and what this project already measured. Labels follow the project's convention.

### Gap 1 — The comparison measured the wrong error (surrogate fidelity versus differentiation method)

* **Evidence in the paper.** Sec. 4.3: no ground truth for HBTO; conclusions "based only on the numerical approximations provided by the four derivative methods". Sec. 4.1: "no derivative information on the feature sets was provided".
* **Literature.** White et al. (2019) trained with a Sobolev norm for this reason; Black & Najafi (2023) compared network derivatives with FEM shape sensitivities. Neither the paper nor, as far as the abstracts show, its follow-up quantifies how the *choice of differentiation method* compares with *surrogate derivative error*.
* **Measured here.** For the published HBTO model the MSE between CFD and NNJ initial sensitivities is 1.07e-4 (and is itself a float32 artefact); on the same filtered sensitivities, the MSE between NNJ and the true sensitivities is 0.064 — about 600 times larger (25 % vs 1.3 % relative). On raw gradients: 0.125 vs 2.8e-4, 444 times. (Corrected 18 November 2026: an earlier "1,160 times" mixed the two bases.) Relative RMS error of the surrogate Jacobian: 78 % (domain), 15 % (interior). A Sobolev-trained admissible network reaches 1.4 % / 0.7 %.
* **Status.** Research opportunity with verified evidence. The ingredients (Sobolev loss, constrained heads, shape-sensitivity references) exist separately; a controlled study that ranks the *sources* of sensitivity error for NN-based TO and follows them to the true compliance of the final design was not found.

### Gap 2 — No noise floor: single-run comparisons of optimization paths

* **Evidence in the paper.** One run per method; ranking by closeness of final designs; differences among NNJ/CSM/AD attributed to accumulated round-off.
* **Literature.** Solver comparisons in TO use performance profiles over large instance sets (Rojas-Labanda & Stolpe 2015–2017; Dolan & Moré 2002).
* **Measured here.** Perturbation runs (`SUMMARY.md` §7): runs that differ only by a 1e-10 relative perturbation of the gradient end in one of two outcomes about 1.5 % apart in compliance; the same happens with the exact SIMP model. Which method lands in which outcome differs between the authors' environment and this one.
* **Status.** Methodological limitation, confirmed. Opportunity: an ensemble protocol (perturbation seeds × instances) as the standard for comparing sensitivity providers.

### Gap 3 — Gradient/objective consistency in NN-based TO pipelines

* **Evidence in the paper.** Sensitivity filter and clipping are used with a general NLP solver; slow convergence is reported and attributed to the trust-region algorithm.
* **Measured here.** Filtered vector vs true gradient: cosine 0.86 (DBTO) and 0.17–0.21 (HBTO) at the authors' final designs; all HBTO runs with a log stop on trust-region collapse.
* **Literature.** Consistency is the stated motivation of Vijayakumaran et al. (2025) for constitutive surrogates; the specific interaction "heuristic filter + clipped surrogate + SQP/interior-point solver" was not found.
* **Status.** Confirmed implementation/methodology issue; the fix (linear filter with chain rule, admissible head) is standard, and its effect was measured only together with a change of optimizer (MMA instead of `trust-constr`): with the published network the designs reach a true compliance of 81.88–82.05, against 82.65–84.72 for the authors' runs (`SUMMARY.md` §6).

### Gap 4 — Precision: the comparison was run in an accidental mixed-precision setting

* **Evidence.** Finding A1. The paper compares methods "to working precision" without controlling the precision of each code path.
* **Measured here.** Error maps for float32/float64 and step size for all methods (`exp_precision.py`, `results/precision.json`): in single precision only the complex step and analytic/AD derivatives stay at round-off (3e-7); central differences need h ≈ 1e-2–1e-3 and reach 5e-5 at best.
* **Status.** Confirmed bug plus a research opportunity of modest size (mixed-precision guidance for surrogate-based optimization on GPUs). No study of this for NN surrogates in TO was found.

### Gap 5 — Higher-order derivatives (direction B)

* **Evidence in the paper.** First derivatives only; AD is said to give "derivatives of arbitrary order".
* **Literature.** Second derivatives of NN constitutive surrogates are used for tangents (Jadoon et al. 2026); second-order solvers help in TO (Rojas-Labanda & Stolpe 2016).
* **Measured here.** Methods: analytic second-order chain rule, hyper-dual AD and complex-step-of-Jacobian agree to 1e-15; second differences of values reach 4e-8 at best and 4e-4 at the paper's h = 1e-6; the complex step gives no advantage for second derivatives taken from values. Surrogate: relative RMS error grows from 0.11 % (value) to 0.22 % (first) to 2.5 % (second derivative) for the published DBTO model.
* **Status.** Research opportunity. Unverified hypothesis: exact surrogate Hessian-vector products make Newton-type HBTO competitive once the surrogate is trained to second order.

### Gap 6 — Architectures (direction A)

* **Measured here.** For smooth networks (deep tanh, residual swish, Fourier features, constrained heads) NNJ = CSM = AD to 1e-15 and CFD(h = 1e-6, float64) to 1e-10: the paper's first-order statement generalizes. For ReLU it does not: CFD is wrong near kinks (7e-3 max error on a trained network) and adaptive differences blend the one-sided slopes with a confident error estimate; the second derivative is zero.
* **Not done here (stated limitation).** CNN, FNO and PINN surrogates were not trained. For field-input surrogates the open question is cost, not accuracy: NNJ, CFD, CSM and forward AD scale with the input dimension, reverse AD does not (measured on MLPs with up to 256 inputs: at 256 inputs reverse AD takes 1.2 ms, the other methods 0.19–1.2 s; `results/efficiency.json`).
* **Status.** Partly closed for MLP-family networks; research opportunity for operator surrogates.

### Gap 7 — Surrogate uncertainty (direction D)

* **Measured here.** A five-member deep ensemble trained on the published data: its spread correlates with the true error (Spearman 0.43 for values, 0.60 for derivatives; `results/hbto_surrogates.json`). The authors' final designs concentrate where the surrogate is least reliable (`figures/fig2_hbto_derivative_error.png`), and their reported compliance is 8.5–10.3 % optimistic.
* **Literature.** Ensemble-driven active learning for optimization surrogates exists in photonics (Pestourie et al.); for parameterized-cell multiscale TO no uncertainty-aware optimization was found in the searches.
* **Status.** Research opportunity; the "exploitation" itself is confirmed, the remedy is an unverified hypothesis.

### Gap 8 — The rectangular-hole parameterization is singular at its boundary

* **Found here (new observation, verified numerically).** As the hole width θ1 → 0 at fixed height θ2 > 0 the cell contains a slit; its converged stiffness tends to that of a cracked cell, not of the solid (e.g. C11 drops from 1.346 to 0.857 for a 5 % × 50 % slit). The voxel data set labels θ < 0.01 as solid, so the training target jumps at the domain edge; the MLP bridges the jump with a steep ramp, which is where its derivative error exceeds 200 %; and 14–20 % of the cells in the published final designs are such slit cells.
* **Status.** Confirmed property of the benchmark; research opportunity (regularized parameterization, minimum feature size as a bound, or a density-aspect parameterization).

### Gaps not pursued (direction C)

3-D, multiple load cases, other boundary conditions, nonlinear and dynamic problems: none was run. The package supports multiple load cases in the FE kernel and a cantilever case, but no result is claimed. These are extensions of scope rather than gaps in the argument, and each is feasible only after gaps 1–3 are closed, because they inherit the same pipeline.
